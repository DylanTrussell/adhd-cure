'''Turkey''' is a country and a bird, and the bird got the name by mistake. The English called the guinea fowl, which reached Europe through Turkish traders, a "turkey cock." When the colonists in America found a similar-looking bird, they gave it the same name.<ref name="oed">''Oxford English Dictionary'', "turkey."</ref>

== The names ==
In French the bird is ''dinde'', short for ''coq d'Inde'', or "chicken of India." In Turkish it is ''hindi'', which means "of India." The bird is from Mexico and the southern United States.<ref name="oed" />

== Franklin ==
Benjamin Franklin did not propose the turkey as the national bird. In a private letter to his daughter in January 1784, written after he had been displeased by an emblem of the Society of the Cincinnati that looked like a turkey to him, he said the bald eagle was "a Bird of bad moral Character" and that the turkey was "a much more respectable Bird." He did not campaign for it.<ref name="franklin">Benjamin Franklin, letter to Sarah Bache, 26 January 1784.</ref>

== Too big ==
Commercially bred turkeys are so heavy-breasted that they generally cannot mate naturally, and the industry uses artificial insemination.<ref name="usda">United States Department of Agriculture, poultry production notes.</ref>

== Wild ==
Wild turkeys fly at up to roughly 55 miles per hour in short bursts. Their numbers fell to about 30,000 in the 1930s from overhunting and habitat loss, and recovered to about 7 million through restocking, according to the National Wild Turkey Federation.<ref name="nwtf">National Wild Turkey Federation, "Wild Turkey History."</ref>

== The pardon ==
Presidents have been given Thanksgiving turkeys since the 1940s. The formal pardon is usually dated to George H. W. Bush in 1989, though accounts of earlier pardons exist.<ref name="whha">White House Historical Association, "Thanksgiving turkey pardon."</ref>

== Tryptophan ==
Turkey does contain tryptophan, but no more than chicken. The sleepiness after Thanksgiving is generally credited to the large meal and the alcohol.

== Türkiye ==
In 2022 the country requested that the UN call it Türkiye. The UN announced the change on 1 June 2022.<ref name="un">United Nations, announcement, 1 June 2022.</ref> The government's stated reasons included that the English word is also the name of a bird, which is the thing the English did first.

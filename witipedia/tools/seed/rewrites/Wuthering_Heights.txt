'''''Wuthering Heights''''' is a novel about two families in which almost everyone is named Catherine, Edgar, Hareton, or Linton, and almost nobody can be trusted as a narrator. Emily Brontë published it in December 1847 under the name Ellis Bell. She died the next December, aged 30, without writing another novel.<ref name="barker">Juliet Barker, ''The Brontës'' (1994).</ref>

== Weather ==
The narrator Lockwood explains the title in the first chapter: "Wuthering" is "a significant provincial adjective, descriptive of the atmospheric tumult to which its station is exposed in stormy weather." The house is named for the wind.

== The reviews ==
Critics were appalled. One review called it a book "in which the reader is shocked, disgusted, almost sickened by details of cruelty and brutality." Another suspected the author was an "unformed" man of crude imagination. Charlotte Brontë later apologised for it in her preface to the 1850 edition, calling the characters "unnatural" while praising the writing, which Emily would not have enjoyed.<ref name="barker" />

== The publisher ==
Thomas Cautley Newby published ''Wuthering Heights'' and Anne Brontë's ''Agnes Grey'' together, with misprints, and later advertised a new Acton Bell novel as the work of Currer Bell, the name Charlotte used. Charlotte and Anne went to London in July 1848 to meet the publisher George Smith and prove they were two different people.<ref name="barker" />

== Heathcliff ==
Heathcliff is never given a surname.

== Kate Bush ==
Kate Bush wrote the song "Wuthering Heights" in 1977 at age 18, in about three hours, after seeing the end of a TV adaptation. She has the same birthday as Emily, 30 July. The single reached number one in the UK in March 1978, making her the first woman to top the UK chart with a song she wrote herself.<ref name="bush">Graeme Thomson, ''Under the Ivy: The Life and Music of Kate Bush'' (2010); Official Charts Company.</ref>

== The 1939 film ==
The best-known film adaptation, directed by William Wyler in 1939, ends halfway through the book. Everything after the first Catherine's death, which is roughly the second half of the novel, is cut.

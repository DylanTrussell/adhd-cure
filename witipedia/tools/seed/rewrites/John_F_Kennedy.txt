'''John F. Kennedy''' was elected president at 43, making him the youngest person ever elected to the office. He was also once rescued from a Pacific island by a message carved into a coconut.<ref name="dallek">Robert Dallek, ''An Unfinished Life: John F. Kennedy 1917-1963'' (2003).</ref>

== PT-109 ==
On 2 August 1943 the Japanese destroyer ''Amagiri'' rammed and cut in half the torpedo boat PT-109, which Kennedy commanded. Two crewmen died. Kennedy towed an injured man by clamping the strap of his life jacket in his teeth and swam for hours to an island.<ref name="dallek" /> Days later, native Solomon Islanders found the crew. Kennedy scratched a message on a coconut: "NAURO ISL / COMMANDER... NATIVE KNOWS POS'IT / HE CAN PILOT / 11 ALIVE / NEED SMALL BOAT / KENNEDY." The husk sat on his desk in the Oval Office.<ref name="lib">John F. Kennedy Presidential Library and Museum, PT-109 collection.</ref>

== The cigars ==
Before signing the Cuban trade embargo in February 1962, Kennedy asked his press secretary Pierre Salinger to get him as many Cuban cigars as he could. Salinger returned with about 1,200 Petit Upmann cigars. Kennedy signed the order.<ref name="salinger">Pierre Salinger, ''With Kennedy'' (1966).</ref>

== The Pulitzer ==
''Profiles in Courage'' won the 1957 Pulitzer Prize for Biography under Kennedy's name. His aide Ted Sorensen did substantial drafting. The extent of the help has been argued ever since; Sorensen said in his 2008 memoir that he "did a first draft of most chapters."<ref name="dallek" />

== "Ich bin ein Berliner" ==
The widely repeated claim that Kennedy called himself a jelly doughnut in West Berlin in 1963 is wrong. "Berliner" means a person from Berlin, and the sentence is grammatical. The pastry is also called a Berliner elsewhere in Germany, but not in Berlin, where it is a ''Pfannkuchen''.<ref name="safire">William Safire, ''Safire's Political Dictionary''.</ref>

'''Bob Dylan''', born Robert Allen Zimmerman in 1941, is the first musician to win the Nobel Prize in Literature. The Swedish Academy gave it to him on 13 October 2016, "for having created new poetic expressions within the great American song tradition."<ref name="nobel">Swedish Academy, Nobel Prize in Literature 2016 announcement.</ref>

== The silence ==
For about two weeks the Academy could not reach him. Permanent secretary Sara Danius called his lack of reply "impolite and arrogant, but that's how he is."<ref name="danius">Sara Danius, interview, Swedish Radio, 21 October 2016.</ref> Dylan told the Academy he would not attend the December ceremony because of "pre-existing commitments," and sent an acceptance speech that the US ambassador to Sweden read aloud at the banquet. Patti Smith sang his song "A Hard Rain's A-Gonna Fall" at the ceremony in his place. Winners must give a lecture within six months of the ceremony to receive the prize money, and he recorded it in June 2017, just before the deadline.<ref name="lecture">Bob Dylan, Nobel Lecture, recorded 5 June 2017.</ref>

== Electric ==
At the Newport Folk Festival on 25 July 1965 he played an electric set. Audience reaction is remembered as a riot of booing; participants dispute how much was booing and how much was complaints about the sound system and the short set.<ref name="heylin">Clinton Heylin, ''Bob Dylan: Behind the Shades'' (2000).</ref> He walked off after three songs.

== The name ==
He changed his name to Dylan around 1961. He has given conflicting accounts of where it came from, at various times denying the Dylan Thomas story and at others saying it just sounded right.<ref name="heylin" />

== The tour ==
He has been on the "Never Ending Tour" since 1988, having played more than 3,000 shows, and has outlasted most of the people who told him to stop.

== The crash ==
In July 1966 he had a motorcycle accident near Woodstock, New York. He disappeared from public view for most of the following year. Details of his injuries were never released.<ref name="shelton">Robert Shelton, ''No Direction Home'' (1986).</ref>

== Whisky ==
He sells a bourbon, a rye, and a double-barrel whiskey under the brand Heaven's Door, launched in 2018.

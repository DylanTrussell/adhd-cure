'''Theodore Roosevelt''' became president at 42 after William McKinley was assassinated, and is still the youngest person to hold the office.<ref name="morris">Edmund Morris, ''The Rise of Theodore Roosevelt'' (1979).</ref>

== The speech ==
On 14 October 1912, while campaigning for a third term, Roosevelt was shot in the chest outside a Milwaukee hotel by a saloonkeeper named John Schrank. Roosevelt noticed that he was not coughing blood, concluded the bullet had not reached his lung, and gave his scheduled speech anyway, for about 90 minutes, bleeding through his shirt. The bullet had passed through his steel eyeglass case and a folded 50-page copy of the speech, which slowed it. Newspapers reported him telling the crowd: "It takes more than that to kill a Bull Moose." The bullet stayed in his chest for the rest of his life.<ref name="trc">Theodore Roosevelt Center, Dickinson State University, "The 1912 assassination attempt."</ref>

== The teddy bear ==
In November 1902, on a hunting trip in Mississippi, aides tied up a captured black bear for him to shoot. He refused. A cartoonist, Clifford Berryman, drew the scene for ''The Washington Post'', and a Brooklyn candy-store owner, Morris Michtom, began selling stuffed "Teddy's bears."<ref name="morris" />

== The pony ==
In 1902 his young son Quentin reportedly smuggled his pony, Algonquin, up the White House elevator to cheer up his sick brother Archie. The family recounted the story. It is probably true, though accounts of the date vary.<ref name="trc" />

== The River of Doubt ==
After losing the 1912 election, Roosevelt led an expedition down an unmapped Amazon tributary in Brazil in 1914. He lost about 50 pounds, got a leg infection, and told his companions to leave him behind. He was carried out.<ref name="millard">Candice Millard, ''The River of Doubt'' (2005).</ref>

== Peace ==
He won the 1906 Nobel Peace Prize for mediating the end of the Russo-Japanese War at Portsmouth, New Hampshire, making him the first American to win a Nobel Prize in any category.<ref name="nobel">Nobel Committee, 1906 Peace Prize announcement.</ref>

== The eye ==
He lost sight in his left eye after a boxing match in the White House in 1904, and kept it secret for years.

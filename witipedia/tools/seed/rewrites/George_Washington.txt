'''George Washington''' was a unanimous choice for president twice and the only president to win every electoral vote. He never lived in the White House, which was not finished until after his death.<ref name="chernow">Ron Chernow, ''Washington: A Life'' (2010).</ref>

== The teeth ==
They were not wooden. By the time of his inauguration he had one natural tooth left. His dentures were assembled from hippopotamus ivory, human teeth, animal teeth, and lead, held together with springs. The dentures were not designed to be comfortable, and the pressure of the springs may account for his tight-lipped expression in portraits.<ref name="mv">Mount Vernon Ladies' Association, "Washington's Teeth."</ref> A Mount Vernon ledger entry of 1784 records a payment to enslaved people for nine teeth.

== The cherry tree ==
There is no cherry tree. The story of Washington confessing that he cut one down ("I cannot tell a lie") first appeared in the fifth edition of Mason Locke Weems's biography in 1806, seven years after Washington's death. Weems invented it.<ref name="weems">Mason Locke Weems, ''The Life of Washington'', 5th ed. (1806).</ref>

== The whiskey ==
In 1797 he built a distillery at Mount Vernon on the advice of his farm manager James Anderson. By 1799 it produced about 11,000 gallons of whiskey, making it one of the largest in the country.<ref name="mv2">Mount Vernon Ladies' Association, "Distillery."</ref>

== Short speech ==
His second inaugural address, in 1793, was 135 words, the shortest in US history.

== The king question ==
After the Revolution some officers suggested he become king. He refused. The story that King George III said "if he does that, he will be the greatest man in the world" comes from an anecdote involving the painter Benjamin West and is unconfirmed.<ref name="chernow" />

== Death ==
After a night in cold rain in December 1799, Washington developed a throat infection. His physicians bled him four times in about twelve hours, removing an estimated 80 ounces, which is roughly 40 percent of his blood. He died on the night of 14 December 1799, aged 67.<ref name="lear">Tobias Lear, journal entry, 14 December 1799.</ref>

== Slavery ==
Washington owned enslaved people throughout his life; 123 lived at Mount Vernon in 1799. His will freed those he owned outright after his wife Martha's death. She freed them in 1801.<ref name="mv3">Mount Vernon Ladies' Association, "Slavery."</ref>

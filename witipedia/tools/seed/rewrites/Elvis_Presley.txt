'''Elvis Presley''' sold more than a billion records, according to his record label, and was a natural blond who dyed his hair black from his teens.<ref name="guralnick">Peter Guralnick, ''Last Train to Memphis'' (1994).</ref>

== The passport ==
His manager, "Colonel" Tom Parker, was a Dutch immigrant named Andreas van Kuijk who entered the United States without papers and never held a US passport. Parker's biographers say this is the most common explanation for why Elvis never toured outside North America, since a foreign tour would have required Parker to leave and try to return.<ref name="nash">Alanna Nash, ''The Colonel'' (2003).</ref>

== The badge ==
On 21 December 1970 Elvis showed up uninvited at the White House with a letter on American Airlines stationery asking to meet Richard Nixon. He wanted a federal narcotics agent's badge. Nixon, after some hesitation, gave him a Bureau of Narcotics and Dangerous Drugs badge. The photograph of the handshake is the most requested photograph in the National Archives.<ref name="archives">US National Archives, "Elvis Meets Nixon" holding record.</ref> At the time Elvis was taking numerous prescription drugs.

== The twin ==
Elvis was born on 8 January 1935. His identical twin brother, Jesse Garon, was stillborn about 35 minutes earlier.<ref name="guralnick" />

== Waist up ==
The claim that television stations only filmed him from the waist up because of his hips is partly true. On his third and last ''Ed Sullivan Show'' appearance on 6 January 1957, cameramen filmed him largely above the waist. Producers said they were responding to complaints from earlier shows, though some shots still show his full body.<ref name="guralnick" />

== Death ==
Elvis died at Graceland on 16 August 1977, at 42. He was found on his bathroom floor. The county medical examiner announced cardiac arrhythmia; later reviewers have disputed the cause and the role of his medications.<ref name="memphis">Shelby County medical examiner's report, 1977, as summarised in Guralnick, ''Careless Love'' (1999).</ref>

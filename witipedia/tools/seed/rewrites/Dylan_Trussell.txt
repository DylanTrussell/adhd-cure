'''Dylan Trussell''' co-founded Culprit Underwear and then wrote a book about it. The book has a discount code.<ref name="intro">Dylan Trussell, ''The Shortcut: How to Start Your Brand with Zero Experience'' (Culprit Press, 2026), Introduction.</ref>

== Credentials ==
In his own words, he is "a college dropout" who has "spoken at Harvard Business School." ''Forbes'' put him on its 2014 30 Under 30 list, spelling his name Trussel. He later notes that other honorees on such lists include Martin Shkreli, Sam Bankman-Fried and Elizabeth Holmes.<ref name="forbes">''Forbes'', "2014 30 Under 30: Marketing & Advertising," 3 December 2013.</ref><ref name="ch118">Trussell, ''The Shortcut'', ch. 118.</ref> The front of his book reads "How to Start Your Brand..." and the next line is "Step one: Don't."<ref name="front">Trussell, ''The Shortcut'', front matter.</ref>

== How it started ==
Twelve colleges rejected him. One accepted him, the University of Colorado Boulder, a school he had not visited. He does not have the degree. "C's get degrees," he writes.<ref name="ch15">Trussell, ''The Shortcut'', ch. 15.</ref>

He and his partner David Dinetz, who ran the film company Culprit Creative, wanted a brand to pay for Sundance. A start-up underwear company liked a pitch about skiing in underwear. They answered with a short film called ''The Wolf of Sundance''. Trussell took his clothes off on a red carpet and was dragged out by security. When the brand's next pitch went nowhere, he said in a parking lot, "Fuck them! We're starting our own underwear brand." Dinetz said the name was good.<ref name="ch1">Trussell, ''The Shortcut'', ch. 1.</ref>

== Budget ==
For a spec Tesla commercial he writes that five people shot it with one camera on a budget of $167, hotel and burgers included. A Tesla-coil technician drew a twelve-foot chalk line past which, he said, the coil would "liquify our heart." The car sat inside the chalk and was struck by lightning until its screens turned on. His father, a lighting director, worked for an In-N-Out burger.<ref name="ch46">Trussell, ''The Shortcut'', ch. 46.</ref>

== Pitch ==
At 24, before a pitch at Fox, he split a bottle of Chardonnay among three to-go Starbucks cups at 8 a.m. He writes that the executives laughed.<ref name="ch67">Trussell, ''The Shortcut'', ch. 67.</ref>

== Numbers ==
Culprit Underwear made two million dollars in its first year and, in his words, later got five million dollars into debt.<ref name="intro" /> ''California Apparel News'' reported in March 2021 that the line began in 2019 as a side hustle.<ref name="apparel">Andrew Asch, ''California Apparel News'', 4 March 2021.</ref>

A chapter titled "Don't Make Swimwear" is chapter 86. In an earlier chapter he tells the reader the swimwear story is in chapter 85. Chapter 85 is "Stay in Your Lane."<ref name="ch86">Trussell, ''The Shortcut'', ch. 72, 85 and 86.</ref> He writes that the swimwear was ready in July, and that swim season starts in January and ends in June.<ref name="ch86" />

A $10,000 Sunset billboard carried no discount code, so no sale could be traced to it. It stayed up ten months. "We used to be really dumb," he writes. "We still are, but we used to be also."<ref name="ch53">Trussell, ''The Shortcut'', ch. 53.</ref>

== Film ==
Dinetz and Trussell directed the 2019 comedy ''Airplane Mode''. Culprit Creative produced it.<ref name="wikiplane">"Airplane Mode (2019 film)," ''Wikipedia''.</ref> He writes that a sale to YouTube Originals fell apart weeks before release, and that it was "like we won the lottery, spent the money, then lost the ticket."<ref name="ch118" />

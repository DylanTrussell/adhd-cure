## 6. The Beatles

### Facts

1. **The audition quote, in the earliest form Quote Investigator could verify.** Brian Epstein, *A Cellarful of Noise* (1964), as checked by Quote Investigator against scans, posted 27 April 2013 and updated through 18 February 2025: “Not to mince words, Mr. Epstein, we don’t like your boys’ sound. Groups of four guitarists are on the way out.” Epstein’s reply in the same passage: “You must be out of your mind. These boys are going to explode. I am completely confident that one day they will be bigger than Elvis Presley.” QI dates the Decca audition to “New Year’s Day in 1962,” from Epstein’s account, and says the wording is Epstein’s memory. https://quoteinvestigator.com/2013/04/27/guitars-out/

2. **The short version is later, and Rowe denied it.** The Independent, Brian Viner, 11 February 2012: the line “Guitar groups are on their way out, Mr Epstein” “were certainly quoted by Epstein, in his 1964 autobiography A Cellarful of Noise. But Rowe went to his grave in 1986 denying it.” “Rowe wasn’t even present at the audition.” Mike Smith “chose to offer a recording contract to the Tremeloes, not least because they hailed from Dagenham.” The Beatles “performed 15 songs.” Mark Lewisohn, as reported there: Rowe “had offered to press their records, but at their expense, not Decca’s,” and Epstein rejected that in “a letter dated Saturday, 10 February 1962.” “George Martin… told Lewisohn that, on the basis of those demos, he would have turned them down, too.” https://www.independent.co.uk/arts-entertainment/music/news/the-man-who-rejected-the-beatles-6782008.html

3. **QI on how the famous wording drifted.** Hunter Davies, *The Beatles: The Authorized Biography* (1968), has Epstein saying “Groups of guitars were on the way out.” The tidy “‘We don’t like their sound. Groups of guitars are on the way out.’ – Decca Recording Company” shows up in Stephen Pile’s *The Book of Heroic Failures* (1979). Quote Investigator, 27 April 2013. https://quoteinvestigator.com/2013/04/27/guitars-out/

4. **Rooftop concert.** Encyclopaedia Britannica, “Today in History—January 30”: “In the early afternoon of January 30, 1969… On the rooftop of Apple Corps headquarters, the Beatles played what would turn out to be their final concert together.” “The 42-minute set—cut short by police intervention.” Songs named: “One After 909,” “Dig a Pony,” “I’ve Got a Feeling,” plus “several renditions of ‘Get Back’ and ‘Don’t Let Me Down’ and an improvised ‘God Save the Queen.’” “3 Savile Row.” “Billy Preston joined the Fab Four.” “arrests were threatened, but no charges were filed.” “210 hours: Length of video and audio recordings from the Let it Be sessions.” https://www.britannica.com/today-in-history/January-30-The-Beatles-Final-Concert

5. **Ed Sullivan audience, official Beatles site.** The Beatles, news item dated 9 February 2016: “The broadcast was watched by an estimated 73 million viewers in over 23 million households - a record for US television, at the time.” https://www.thebeatles.com/9th-february-1964-beatles-make-their-ed-sullivan-show-debut

6. **The rating, the studio, the caption.** Ed Sullivan Show artist page: “The television rating was a record-setting 45.3… 23,240,000 American homes.” “a 60 share.” “CBS’s TV Studio 50… could only accommodate an audience of 700.” “over 50,000 requests for seats.” At 8 o’clock, 9 February 1964. When the camera reached John Lennon, “the caption below his name also read ‘SORRY GIRLS, HE’S MARRIED.’” Ringo Starr, asked “How do you find America?”: “Turn left at Greenland.” Handshake booking: “On Monday, November 11, 1963 Epstein met Sullivan at the Delmonico Hotel.” https://www.edsullivan.com/artists/the-beatles/

7. **Newsweek’s prediction, as quoted on that page.** February 24, 1964 issue, quoted by the Ed Sullivan Show site: “Visually, they are a nightmare: tight, dandified, Edwardian/Beatnik suits and great pudding bowls of hair.” The quoted review “ended with… ‘the odds are they will fade away, as most adults confidently predict.’” https://www.edsullivan.com/artists/the-beatles/

8. **Set list that night, same page.** “All My Loving,” “Till There Was You,” “She Loves You,” then later “I Saw Her Standing There” and “I Want to Hold Your Hand.” https://www.edsullivan.com/artists/the-beatles/

### True but funny
